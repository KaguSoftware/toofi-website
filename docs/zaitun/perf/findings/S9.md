# S9 findings: device tiers and gating

Search track S9 of the [optimization plan](../../OPTIMIZATION_PLAN.md), 2026-11-22. Scope: whether the capability check puts real devices in the right tier, the frame-rate steps between quality levels, Save-Data and slow networks, low battery, background tabs, and heat on long sessions. Files read: `scene/capability.ts`, `experience/quality.ts`, `ExperienceRoot.tsx`, `ExperienceCanvas.tsx`, `ZaitunExperience.tsx`, `scene/SceneViewport.tsx`, and drei 10.7.9's `PerformanceMonitor.js` (read from `node_modules`).

**Status: complete, awaiting approval.** The three timed rig runs (`live.mjs network | adapt | pixels`) ran on the charger under the lock, 2026-11-22 17:05–17:14. Results: `scripts/perf/results/2026-11-22T14-05-29-S9-network`, `…14-07-28-S9-adapt`, `…14-12-37-S9-pixels` (git-ignored).

## The short version

- **The tier is decided from screen width, thread count and memory. None of them says how fast the GPU is.** Run through the shipped functions, a Pixel 7 held sideways (915 px wide, 8 GB, 8 threads) gets the high tier: the 10.26 MB model, a 4096² shadow map, levels 1–3. So does a 4-core / 8-thread Intel UHD laptop and an 8-core iGPU desktop on a 4K monitor. The high tier can never go below level 1 (S9-1, S9-2, S9-7).
- **A software renderer gets the 3D scene.** Chrome hands out a WebGL2 context on SwiftShader, also with `failIfMajorPerformanceCaveat: true`, so neither the probe in `capability.ts` nor that flag can tell it from a GPU. The renderer string can (S9-3).
- **The quality monitor is dead after about 11 s and cannot react to heat.** Measured: with the CPU slowed 12× at 30 s, the frame rate fell from 60 to 35.9 fps for 50 s and the level never moved off 2 (S9-4). A slow desktop that never used up its flips did step down, after 39 s.
- **A normal 4G link in Iraq is refused.** Chrome reports `effectiveType` "3g" for a 5 Mbps link with 300 ms round trip (4g at 150 ms), and the site then shows the stills only. Measured (S9-11).
- **A 4K or 5K canvas costs 3–4× a laptop's.** Same camera and level: 2.7 MP → 14 ms (relative GPU timer), 8.0 MP → 50 ms; at level 3 a 4K canvas falls to 30 fps (S9-7).
- **Visitors the check turns away have no way back in.** Save-Data, slow networks, under 4 GB and under 4 threads get the stills with no button, while a reduced-motion visitor gets a "Load 3D" offer (S9-6).
- **Background tabs are handled; battery needs no code.** Frames stop when the tab is hidden and the monitor shrugs off the gap (S9-8, S9-9).

## Ruled out

- **The phone profile at 16× CPU as a weak-phone test** (it held 58.7 fps): the phone tier's limit is the GPU, which only the real phone can show. S4 reached the same conclusion for the low tier.

- **`failIfMajorPerformanceCaveat: true` as the software-rendering test.** Chrome 157 returns a SwiftShader context regardless (S9-3). Use the renderer string.
- **Re-deciding the tier on resize or rotation.** The model is fixed at mount; the cost of a mismatch is S9-1's, fixed at the source.
- **Battery Status API** (S9-9).

## For the owner (at T)

1. May the high tier fall to level 0 (no shadows) and to the stills when even that is slow? This changes what weak laptops see; strong ones are unaffected.
2. May a 4K monitor render below native resolution at level 2 and 3? (S9-7.)
4. Should a visitor on a 3g-reported link get the 3D (with an offer) or the stills? (S9-11, and the unanswered question of whether "under 5 s" means content or live scene.)
3. Wording and placement of the "Load 3D" offer for refused visitors (S9-6); the plan's targets say nothing about them.

## Rig notes for P0
